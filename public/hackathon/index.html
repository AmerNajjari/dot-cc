Part 2 · Identify the Value

Score your idea

Screen ideas and gaps in a sector. Benchmark what already exists. Then prioritize with a scorecard:

CriterionThe questionScore 1 to 5
PainHow badly do people complain about this gap?
DemandHow many people have it?
Market trendIs it growing or shrinking?
Market share potentialHow much can you realistically capture?
Threats and alternativesWill something replace this category? (e.g., AI agents replacing SaaS)

What AI changes: Screening and benchmarking take hours, not months. Confirming the pain still needs real conversations.

Teams already have an idea, so they score their own idea, 1 to 5, in 3 minutes.

NABC: your first conversation with AI

N · Need

Who exactly has the problem? How painful is it? How do they solve it today?

A · Approach

What is your unique solution and business model?

B · Benefits per cost

What does the customer gain compared with what they pay and give up?

C · Competition

Who else solves it, including the option of doing nothing?

“Act as a critical startup advisor. Challenge my idea using NABC. Tell me what is weak and which of the four is my biggest risk.”

NABC was developed at SRI International (originally Stanford Research Institute) and championed by its former CEO Curtis Carlson. It is also a pitch structure, so teams can reuse it in the final pitch.

Part 2 · Think like a whole company

One founder, six heads

Sales

Marketing

Financial head

What does it cost, what do we charge, and when do we break even?

Legal advisor

Part 3 · Who is an entrepreneur?

An entrepreneur is someone who spots unmet needs, connects the dots others miss, influences people to believe in a vision and join it, then acts and persists until the idea creates real value.

رائد الأعمال هو من يكتشف الاحتياجات غير الملبّاة، ويربط النقاط التي لا يراها غيره، ويؤثر في الناس ليؤمنوا برؤيته وينضموا إليها، ثم يبادر ويثابر حتى تتحول الفكرة إلى قيمة حقيقية.

FR

Part 4 · A thinking skill for life

The Six Thinking Hats

One hat at a time. Everyone wears the same hat, then you switch.

White hatFACTS

What do we know? What is missing?

Red hatFEELINGS

Gut reaction. No reasons needed.

Black hatRISKS

What could go wrong?

Yellow hatBENEFITS

Why will it work?

Green hatIDEAS

What else could we try?

Blue hatPROCESS

What do we decide, and what is next?

Edward de Bono, Six Thinking Hats (1985). Photo: Impact Hub, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

One click per hat. The idea: a team argues less and thinks better when everyone looks in the same direction at the same time. Instead of one person defending and another attacking, the whole team wears the white hat together, then the red, and so on. The blue hat runs the session: it opens, keeps time and closes with a decision. Students can use this for any decision later, not only for an MVP.

Part 4 · Master prompt

The master prompt: what it gives you

1Idea snapshot and your 3 riskiest assumptions

2NABC check, starting from your Prompt 1 answers

3Market and customers: behaviour, size, your competitor analysis, white space

4Business Model Canvas

5Six-heads review: sales, marketing, finance, legal, IT and security, business development

6MVP: one user, one core flow, max 5 features, a success metric, a stop rule

7A ready-to-paste build prompt, secure by default

8Test plan, and the input for your 100-customer test

The AI plays a board of six heads, the same six from Part 2, plus a chair who gives a Go, Change or Stop verdict. Every number must carry a source or be labelled Estimate or Assumption. Legal covers registration, licences, the Personal Data Protection Law, consumer rules, VAT and e-invoicing, and ends with the questions to ask a real lawyer. The build prompt is secure by default: proper login, no keys in the code, users see only their own data, minimum personal data.

Part 4 · Trust, but check

Check 3 numbers yourself

AI writes numbers that look right. Some are wrong, and some are invented.

Why

AI can make things up

Ask for a competitor’s price, funding or market size, and a chatbot may give a confident number that no source ever said. One wrong number can sink a pitch.

Which 3

The 3 your decision rests on

1. Your closest competitor’s price

2. The size of your market

3. The number you will say in your pitch

How · 1 minute each

Open, find, compare

1. Open the source link.

2. Find the exact number on the page.

3. Check the year and the country.

No link, or the number is not on the page? Delete it, or label it “estimate”. Never pitch a number you could not find.

What “check 3 numbers” means: AI chat tools sometimes produce facts that sound right but are false, most often exact figures such as prices, funding rounds, user counts and market sizes. Checking everything is impossible in a workshop, so each team checks only the 3 numbers their decision depends on. A number passes if the linked page really shows it, it is recent, and it is about the right country. Prompt 2 ends by listing its 3 most important numbers with links, which makes this step fast.
